# Layout Routing

Scope: Explicit offline renderer operation or implementation. Enter through [Offline workflow](offline-workflow.md) for the local runtime contract.

Use this reference after source classification and before authoring candidate composition grammars. It converts a mixed upload set into three modern, materially coherent layout routes without treating every image as an equal card.

## Core distinction

`layoutFamily` is a renderer capacity contract. A capacity family is not an art direction. It answers how many approved content assets must remain legible; it does not decide the visual language.

`layoutRouting` is the authored visual decision. It records one shared surface style and three candidate-specific routes. Each route owns its archetype, density, alignment, scale rhythm, rationale, and complete relative-only `compositionGrammar`.

EDC is one route, not the default. Everyday-carry references are useful for edge alignment, scale hierarchy, stable gutters, and inventory rhythm. They do not authorize copying a photographed set, its labels, its brands, or its selection of objects. Do not copy labels, brands, wording, or object selection.

## Read the upload set before routing

Route from evidence, in this order:

1. `asset_count` — count only approved visible content; references and exclusions do not enter the layout.
2. `role_mix` — identify the one hero, then secondary identity/evidence and supporting context.
3. `representation_mix` — distinguish isolated objects, bounded photographs, drawings, material fields, contact prints, film, tracing layers, relief, and exact cutouts.
4. `alpha_mix` — true transparency permits object-like placement; opaque uploads need truthful carriers.
5. `aspect_mix` — note repeated portrait, landscape, square, panoramic, and very narrow forms.
6. `series_cohesion` — decide whether sources are one consistent series or a deliberately heterogeneous archive.
7. `text_presence` — reserve a title card or annotation channel only for separately approved text.
8. `palette_evidence` and `material_evidence` — choose a unifying surface without flattening exact colors or material behavior.
9. `user_direction` — preserve explicit hierarchy, placement, crop, spacing, and style instructions.

Record every signal actually used in `selectionSignals`. Do not cite a signal merely to make a route sound intentional.

## Modern layout constants

Every accepted route follows these rules:

- Use one dominant alignment system: optical axis, edge grid, spine, band, or radial constellation.
- Establish three scale levels when the count permits: hero, secondary, and supporting. Avoid equal-card flattening.
- Use stable gutters and optical correction. A narrow tool, square card, and wide photograph should not receive equal bounding boxes.
- Prefer variable module spans over a uniform thumbnail matrix. Large modules carry identity; small modules carry evidence or cadence.
- Preserve native object orientation unless rotation has a clear reading or contact purpose.
- Let crop, edge contact, sleeve, tracing overlap, or partial off-frame placement express physical relation only when the asset contract permits it.
- Keep one coherent negative-space strategy. Empty space is structural; it is not a vacancy to decorate.
- For one-to-six sources, target the weighted optical centroid near the safe-field center before treating any gap as available. Weight area by hierarchy, contrast, and opacity; do not average object centers or force mirror symmetry.
- Classify large gaps as `functional_breathing_space`, `recoverable_by_recomposition`, or `supplement_candidate`. The routing order is `recompose before supplement`; a P3/P4 candidate cannot compensate for a P1 balance failure.
- Keep all approved content visible and legible at output size. If the route cannot meet the minimum short edge, choose another compatible route or block.
- Keep one shared light direction and contact field. Distinct materials retain distinct transmission, roughness, edge, and shadow behavior.
- Never turn opaque photographs into floating object stickers. Never place reference-only pixels in the output.

## Deterministic route decision matrix

Apply hard count compatibility first, then score the remaining archetypes in this order: semantic relationship, representation and alpha fit, aspect fit, hero legibility, series coherence, and explicit user direction. A fashionable reference never outranks source truth or the user's approved hierarchy.

| Observed approved-content pattern | Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| one dominant source with at most two quiet explanations | `singular_stage` | an underfilled inventory grid |
| two sources that explain each other | `paired_dialogue` | false symmetry between unrelated items |
| sparse heterogeneous sources around one unmistakable hero | `asymmetric_constellation` | four-corner postage stamps |
| four to twenty isolated objects or truthful carriers that form one inventory | `modular_inventory` | equal boxes and arbitrary row fill |
| mixed portrait, landscape, document, and object evidence with a reading sequence | `editorial_spine` | a decorative spine with no hierarchy |
| process frames, film, contact prints, or sequential evidence | `evidence_ribbon` | unrelated objects forced into a strip |
| product and evidence connected by an evidenced material plane | `material_stage` | invented generic material scenery |
| nine or more comparable items with real categories | `dense_taxonomy` | shrinking heterogeneous sources into icons |

For each compatible archetype, reject it if the minimum visible short edge cannot survive, the hero stops being unmistakable, or a truthful carrier would be misrepresented. Select three distinct survivors. If fewer than three survive, stop and report the conflict instead of fabricating superficial alternatives.

## Archetype catalog

### `singular_stage` — 1 to 3 visible assets

Use `optical_axis` alignment. Suitable for a single exact product, or a product with one or two quiet pieces of evidence. The frame should read as one event, not an underfilled grid. With one visible source, the product remains the only visual level; density and scale-rhythm metadata may vary across the three required candidate signatures without inventing supporting content.

Unless a user-directed offset or named reading channel requires otherwise, place the event so its optical centroid—not merely its geometry box—reads near the safe-field center. Keep surrounding air continuous and purposeful.

Reject when the supporting assets are a taxonomy that needs comparison.

### `paired_dialogue` — 2 to 6 visible assets

Use an optical axis for two unequal but mutually explanatory masses: product and process, object and drawing, or object and material sample. One side remains dominant. Contact or a measured gap must explain the relationship.

First adjust scale, distance, and shared axis until the pair reads as one centered visual sentence. A remaining empty side is not a reason to invent a third noun; it becomes a `supplement_candidate` only when the pair is already balanced and the subject loses a necessary link without the addition.

Reject when the pair has no semantic relationship or would falsely imply equal identity.

### `asymmetric_constellation` — 2 to 16 visible assets

Use radial alignment, one anchored hero, and controlled satellites with progressive scale. The constellation is optically balanced, not four-corner decoration. Satellites follow semantic adjacency and varied distance; they do not become postage stamps.

Reject when a dense set needs strict comparison or when the hero cannot remain unmistakable.

### `modular_inventory` — 4 to 20 visible assets

Use `edge_grid` alignment with unequal modules, variable module spans, and a modular scale rhythm. This is the EDC-adjacent route: align real silhouettes and truthful carriers to shared edges while letting larger or more important forms span additional modules. Labels are optional and require separate text approval.

Reject a uniform card grid, identical object boxes, arbitrary row filling, or an inventory that hides product hierarchy.

### `editorial_spine` — 3 to 40 visible assets

Use a vertical spine and a hero-dominant scale rhythm. Place the hero or approved title event on the spine; attach evidence in controlled left/right excursions. Use it for mixed portrait and landscape sources, documents, or a narrative archive that needs a clear reading sequence.

Reject when every item must be compared simultaneously or when the spine becomes a decorative line with no reading function.

### `evidence_ribbon` — 3 to 40 visible assets

Use a horizontal band with progressive scale. A hero sits above, below, or interrupts the ribbon; the `evidence_band` group carries contact prints, process frames, drawings, or sequence evidence. Keep the ribbon physically plausible as film, paper, or aligned objects.

Reject when the evidence is unrelated, too tall for a band, or reduced below legibility.

### `material_stage` — 2 to 16 visible assets

Use one optical axis and a `material_bridge` group. A restrained plane, field, sheet, or platform connects product and supporting content through a real material relationship. This route suits metal with acrylic, product with paper relief, or an object with an authorized material study.

Reject when the material is invented, generic, or merely a colored background.

### `dense_taxonomy` — 9 to 40 visible assets

Use a compact edge grid with multiple unequal groups and strong category rhythm. Reserve larger spans for identity-bearing assets and smaller repeated spans for comparable evidence. Keep group gutters larger than item gutters.

Reject when the output size cannot preserve minimum legibility or when the set is too heterogeneous to form meaningful categories.

## Surface-style catalog

Select one shared `surfaceStyle` for the three candidates so layout can be compared without changing the entire art direction.

Choose that shared style from evidence, not taste alone:

| Evidence pattern | Preferred surface style | Blocking caution |
|---|---|---|
| mixed exact colors or uncertain material context | `luminous_neutral` | do not recolor exact sources |
| jewelry, precision metal, industrial components, or dark edge structure | `graphite_precision` | retain readable dark-on-dark separation |
| approved paper, amber film, wood, or leather relationships | `warm_archive` | do not fake age or provenance |
| one evidenced accent color suitable for a bounded translucent plane | `chromatic_acrylic` | one accent family, not color islands |
| drawings, measurements, process diagrams, or restrained linework | `monochrome_technical` | do not invent labels, units, or claims |
| real film, tracing, contact-print, or transmissive representations | `translucent_film` | do not make opaque sources translucent |

Tie-break in this order: exact-source readability, approved user direction, material evidence, palette evidence, then viewing context. If the user explicitly requests style exploration, complete a separately approved routing pass; do not quietly vary surface style inside the three-layout comparison.

### `luminous_neutral`

Cool-to-neutral frosted lightbox, low chroma, restrained falloff, and exact source colors. Use for mixed-color assets or when fidelity is the dominant requirement.

### `graphite_precision`

Graphite surround, silver or dark tools and carriers, controlled reflective highlights, and clear edge contrast. Use for technical, jewelry, industrial, or precision narratives when dark structure helps the exact product remain legible.

### `warm_archive`

Warm paper, amber film, wood, leather, or aged neutral relationships under calibrated soft light. Use only when the sources or approved direction support archival warmth; do not simulate false age.

### `chromatic_acrylic`

One bounded accent hue expressed through acrylic or glass while the remaining field stays quiet. Use when exact source color or approved palette evidence can support the accent. Avoid multiple unrelated saturated blocks.

### `monochrome_technical`

Near-monochrome paper, tracing, linework, and precise metal contrast. Use for diagrams, process studies, measurement, or blueprint-like evidence without inventing labels or scientific claims.

### `translucent_film`

Transmitted film, tracing sheets, contact prints, and layered opacity under one light table. Use when the representation plan contains real transmissive carriers. It is not permission to make every asset translucent.

## Three-candidate protocol

Produce exactly one route for each supported template:

- `archive_research_v1` explores system and evidence organization.
- `product_focus_v1` protects the strongest product event and the most negative space.
- `art_lab_v1` explores the most expressive evidence-backed material or sequence relation.

The three route signatures must differ. Do not create three seed variations of the same layout. A signature is the combination of `archetype`, `density`, `alignment`, and `scaleRhythm`. Each candidate receives its own complete composition grammar and must cover every required visible source exactly once.

Choose compatible archetypes by count, then maximize meaningful contrast:

- 1 source: three distinct `singular_stage` signatures using density and scale-rhythm differences.
- 2 sources: prefer `paired_dialogue`, `asymmetric_constellation`, and `material_stage` when material evidence exists.
- 3 sources: prefer `asymmetric_constellation`, `editorial_spine`, and `evidence_ribbon`; substitute `material_stage` when justified.
- 4–8 sources: compare `modular_inventory`, `editorial_spine`, and `evidence_ribbon` or `asymmetric_constellation`.
- 9–16 sources: compare `modular_inventory`, `editorial_spine`, and `dense_taxonomy` or `evidence_ribbon`.
- 17–20 sources: compare `modular_inventory`, `editorial_spine`, and `dense_taxonomy`.
- 21–40 sources: compare `editorial_spine`, `evidence_ribbon`, and `dense_taxonomy`.

These are routing defaults, not aesthetic permission. Replace a default when actual aspect, role, carrier, series, or user evidence makes another compatible route stronger.

## Candidate grammar requirements

For every candidate:

1. The root is the approved integration hero.
2. Reading order starts at the hero and contains every required visible asset once.
3. Groups form one complete, contiguous partition.
4. Every non-root asset has exactly one earlier parent.
5. The archetype and flow agree: `editorial_spine` is vertical; `modular_inventory` and `evidence_ribbon` are horizontal; `asymmetric_constellation` is radial.
6. `evidence_ribbon` includes an `evidence_band`; `material_stage` includes a `material_bridge`; `modular_inventory` and `dense_taxonomy` include a multi-asset group.
7. Negative space is authored as a breathing field, directional channel, or perimeter relief.
8. The grammar remains `relative-only`. Coordinates come only from confirmed user markup or coordinates, or from the deterministic solver without relabeling them as user evidence.

## Visual rejection checklist

Reject a candidate before preview approval when any answer is yes:

- Does it look like a large background rectangle containing smaller pasted rectangles?
- Are sources distributed to four corners merely to fill space?
- Are most assets the same scale despite different roles and shapes?
- Is a product photograph pretending to be an isolated object?
- Are carriers floating without contact, shared light, or material support?
- Does color break into unrelated islands with no dominant material relation?
- Is an EDC grid used because it is fashionable rather than because the assets form an inventory?
- Are labels, numbers, or title cards present without exact text approval?
- Did any reference-only subject, brand, wording, object choice, or recognizable composition leak into the scene?
- Would removing an element leave identity, evidence, hierarchy, linkage, balance, and material transition unchanged?

Passing this checklist authorizes a preview candidate, not publication. Inspect actual pixels at the second human gate.
